# Lessons 2.4-2.5: Income Statement, Statement of Changes in Equity and Cash Flow Statement

Income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement. The lessons cover the objectives, structure and composition of the income statement, the two ways of preparing it (by nature and by function), and the links with the balance sheet. They then present the statement of changes in equity, the cash flow statement, the notes to the financial statements and the management report.

## Income Statement

The purpose of the income statement is to determine the economic result generated by a company in a given financial year. Unlike the balance sheet, which is a photograph of the equity position at a given moment, the income statement is a flow document that aggregates revenues and costs from the first to the last day of the financial year. It explains how the company's assets were used to produce goods and services, how these were sold and what the difference is between the sales value and the costs to produce them, defined as the "result for the year" or "profit for the year".

**Key Points:**
- It is a multi-step document, with a single section
- It answers the question of what the economic result of a financial year was
- It is a flow document that covers the entire financial year (e.g. 1 January – 31 December)
- The difference between the sales value of the goods/services and the costs to produce them is the "result for the year"
- It presents a division between "continued operations" and "discontinued operations"

**Explanation:**
There are two ways of preparing it: by nature and by function.

1. **Income statement by nature:** costs are aggregated according to their nature (personnel costs, raw materials, etc.). It is the most widely used method in Italy and is provided for by both the international accounting standards and the civil legislation. The structure starts from revenues and subtracts the operating costs (personnel, raw materials, etc.) to obtain the operating profit. Below it, the results of the financial and extraordinary management are added to arrive at the profit before tax, and finally, after taxes, at the net profit.
2. **Income statement by function (or cost of sales):** costs are aggregated according to their functional purpose. It is allowed by the international accounting standards, but not by the Italian ones. It isolates the revenues and costs strictly tied to production to calculate the "gross industrial margin". Subsequently, the support costs (distribution, administration) are subtracted and other operating revenues are added to obtain the operating profit. From there on, the structure is identical to that of the by-nature model.

**Example:**
Company A owns a share of Company B's share capital. When Company B makes a profit and distributes a dividend, part of it goes to Company A.

**Calculation Steps:**
- The dividend received by Company A is not recorded in the core activity or in the operating profit
- It is instead recorded in the financial activity section of the income statement

## Gross Industrial Margin

It is the profit generated with reference only to the direct costs of production and only to the revenues from the sale of the goods and services produced. The algebraic sum of the gross industrial margin and the other cost and revenue items not directly related to production (distribution costs, administrative expenses, other operating revenues) determines the operating profit.

---

## Revenues

The revenues item includes all the revenues from core operations, i.e. the sale of goods and the provision of services. It also includes other operating revenues not related to core operations.

**Key Points:**
- **Revenues from core operations:** derive from the sale of goods and the provision of services produced by the company
- **Other operating revenues:** related to the business activity but not to core operations. Examples include rental income, compensation for damages, contractual penalties, and capital gains from the sale of tangible and intangible assets

**Example:**
If the company sells a machine whose book value is 50 for 60, the difference of 10 represents a capital gain.

**Calculation Steps:**
- The capital gain of 10 (60 − 50) is allocated among the "other revenues", not in the core activity, but still among the operating revenues

---

## Operating Costs

These are the costs necessary to carry out the company's activity. They include costs related to the core activity and other operating costs.

**Key Points:**
- Costs of raw materials
- Costs of services purchased externally (e.g. consulting, outsourced services)
- **Depreciation:** the portion of the durable asset that is conventionally consumed in the production activity of the year
- Changes in the value of non-current assets (e.g. a sudden loss of value of a machine)
- Personnel costs (wages, salaries, ancillary charges, TFR)

**Example:**
A company has a machine worth 10 with a useful life of 5 years.

**Calculation Steps:**
- In each of the 5 years, the annual depreciation (equal to 2 in this case) is reported among the operating costs. This serves to correctly represent the overall amount of resources used and to spread the cost of the multi-year asset over the years in which it takes part in the economic activity, following the economic accrual principle

---

## Financial Income and Expenses

They relate to the financing activity needed to sustain the business, not to the core production activity.

**Key Points:**
- **Profits from investee companies:** if Company A holds an interest in Company B and B distributes dividends, these are recorded as financial income for A
- **Financial income from investment activities:** interest income accrued from equity funds or other financial assets
- **Financial expenses and losses:** interest expense on loans, and lasting losses in the value of financial assets (e.g. equity investments, bonds)

---

## Scheme of the Economic Result

It schematically represents the company's economic result.

**Explanation:**
1. It starts from revenues and operating costs, whose difference gives the operating profit or result (net operating margin), which indicates the company's ability to generate value through its operating activity.
2. Financial income is added to the net operating margin and financial expenses are subtracted, obtaining the profit before tax.
3. Taxes are calculated on the profit before tax.
4. Subtracting taxes gives the net profit from continued operations, the main figure for reading the company's economic health in the year.

---

## Discontinued Operations

Activities whose performance will cease within the next financial year.

**Key Points:**
- The revenues and costs connected to these activities must be shown separately in the income statement, below the net profit from continued operations
- The aim is to show how much they affect the economic result and to indicate that they will no longer contribute in the future

**Example:**
In year T a company resolves to dispose of a business unit in year T+1.

**Calculation Steps:**
- The costs and revenues connected to that business unit in year T must be shown separately in the income statement, with a clear indication that from year T+1 they will no longer contribute to the overall economic result

---

## Earnings per Share (EPS)

Information that companies with shares traded on the financial markets must provide in the income statement.

**Key Points:**
- It is calculated as the ratio between the total profit that will be distributed as a dividend and the average number of shares outstanding during the financial year
- It serves to give the reader of the financial statements an indication of the expected return of a single share
- The actual outcome depends on the company's dividend distribution choices

---

## Link Between the Income Statement and the Balance Sheet

The result for the year, the last item of the income statement, is the item that makes the accounts balance on the liabilities and equity side of the balance sheet.

**Key Points:**
- It represents the way in which the company self-finances
- A positive economic result allows the company's overall equity to grow over time
- The balance between assets and liabilities and equity derives from the calculation of the net profit for the year as determined in the income statement

---

## Statement of Changes in Equity

The objective of this document is to provide information on the various transactions that directly affect equity, analyzing how it changes from year T-1 to year T to which the financial statements refer.

**Key Points:**
- Changes due to the impairment of non-current assets
- Changes in fair value that are not recognized in the income statement (e.g. revaluation or write-down of fixed assets)
- Effects of changes in accounting standards
- Correction of any errors in the preparation of the financial statements
- It shows charges or income that were allocated directly to equity, starting from the profit or loss for the year

---

## Cash Flow Statement

Accounting document that represents the financial flows related to the company's activity in a given financial year, recorded according to the financial cash principle. It is mandatory under the international accounting standards (called the cash flow statement), unlike under the Italian civil legislation.

**Key Points:**
- The international accounting standards do not provide a rigid scheme but a minimum content
- The financial flows from operating activities, investing activities and financing activities must be represented separately
- **Operating flows:** cash inflows/outflows related to the revenue- and cost-generating activity (e.g. sale of goods, acquisition of resources, collection of receivables, payment of debts)
- **Investing flows:** flows related to the purchase or disposal of fixed assets and other financial investments
- **Financing flows:** flows related to transactions that change own capital or third-party financing (e.g. taking on/repaying financial debts)

**Explanation:**
The representation is allowed with two alternative methods: direct or indirect. The direct method, suggested by the international standards, represents in aggregate form the positive and negative flows classified in the three items. The indirect method, mandatory in the Italian civil system, reconstructs the financial flow starting from the result for the year (based on economic accrual) and making adjustments. Although the schemes differ, the final balances of the three areas of activity (operating, investing, financing) are the same with both methods. The cash flow statement calculates the cash flow for each of the three areas, then the overall financial flow as their algebraic sum, and finally reconstructs the cash available at the end of the year starting from the initial one.

## Notes to the Financial Statements

A set of technical information on the principles of preparation of the financial statements, on the accounting standards chosen and on the detailed elements of each item of the financial statements. It is the technical document that explains the content of the individual items of the financial statements.

**Key Points:**
- It contains a declaration of compliance with the chosen international accounting standards (IFRS)
- It includes a summary of the main accounting criteria adopted
- It reports technical details such as the gross value of fixed assets, the accumulated depreciation fund, the principles for determining the depreciation rate
- It may contain non-financial information related to the management's strategies and the company's risk potential

---

## Management Report

Although not an integral part of the financial statements under the international standards, it is often included and contains a qualitative and quantitative analysis of important factors that determined the company's economic result.

**Key Points:**
- Describes the evolution of the reference market and the strategy adopted by the company
- Illustrates the investment policy and the financing (debt) policies
- Describes the criteria for remunerating the invested capital (e.g. dividends)
- On Consob's request, it must include a reclassification of the financial statements showing the relationship between national and international accounting standards
- On Consob's request, it must describe atypical or unusual transactions
- On Consob's request, it must report alternative performance indicators, including non-financial ones
