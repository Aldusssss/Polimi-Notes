# Lessons 4.3-4.4: Investment Evaluation Process and Net Present Value

## Topic Overview

The lessons illustrate the decision-making process through which management evaluates the economic convenience of an investment (e.g. the purchase of a new machine). They cover the preliminary steps: identification of the alternatives (mandatory and non-mandatory investments), definition of the consistency of the project (bundling/unbundling), delimitation of the boundaries of the analysis and evaluation of the competitive impacts. They then go into the main criterion of economic evaluation, the Net Present Value (NPV), with its formula, key components (net cash flows, discount rate, time horizon, terminal value) and decision criterion (NPV > 0 indicates convenience). The importance of estimating these elements accurately is stressed, often with the support of specialized consultants.

---

## 1. Decision-Making Process for Evaluating Investments

### Steps of the decision-making process

- To evaluate the convenience of an investment, management follows a structured process
- The steps are: 1) identification of the decision alternatives; 2) verification of the consistency of the alternatives; 3) definition of the boundaries of the project analysis; 4) analysis of the competitive impacts
- Only after these steps does it proceed to the economic evaluation, calculating the net cash flows

### Identification of the decision alternatives

- Investments are divided into non-mandatory and mandatory
- **Non-mandatory investments:** the main alternative is not to invest (e.g. not to buy the machine). It is wrong to assume that not investing keeps the results unchanged: it often reduces the company's ability to generate positive results (e.g. wear of the existing machine)
- **Mandatory investments:** the obligation is mainly economic (not legal) when the investment is necessary to ensure business continuity (e.g. replacing a machine at the end of its useful life). There are also legal obligations, such as adjustments to anti-pollution or safety regulations

### Identification of the consistency of the project

- The "consistency of the project" includes all the elements that make up the investment
- A main project (e.g. purchase of a machine) can include related subprojects (e.g. hiring specialized staff, purchasing software licenses)
- **Bundling:** including in the perimeter all the related and necessary projects
- **Unbundling:** separating out non-essential activities in order to evaluate the consistency correctly

### Definition of the boundaries of the analysis

- The impact of the investment on resources, processes and output must be considered
- Examples: reconfiguration of the plant layout, change of raw material suppliers, redefinition of staff skills
- These "external boundaries" must be included in the convenience evaluation

### Analysis of the competitive impacts

- It is assessed how the investment affects the economic value of the company or of a specific activity
- Possible impacts: ability to produce innovative products/services, process efficiency, reduction of operating rigidity (greater flexibility), widening of production possibilities (e.g. more types of product with a new machine), automation with cost reduction

## 2. Economic Evaluation Criteria: Net Present Value (NPV)

### Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) methodology

- Family of criteria that takes into account the change in the value of money over time
- Future cash flows are discounted to their value today for a correct comparison

### Net Present Value (NPV)

- The best-known and most widely used criterion for evaluating investments
- It is the sum of the net cash flows generated by the investment, discounted at a discount rate (k), over a defined time horizon
- The net cash flow for each period (t) is the difference between the net operating flows and the investments made in that period

### Formula and components of the NPV

- The NPV is the sum, from t = 0 to T (time horizon), of the discounted net cash flows, plus the (discounted) terminal value
- **Time horizon (T):** finite period defined by management, not infinite
- **Terminal value (TV):** residual value of the investment at the end of T, capturing the potential for value beyond that date
- **Decision criterion:** the investment is convenient if NPV > 0; a positive NPV represents the overall return generated, expressed in present terms

### Elements to estimate for the NPV

To calculate the NPV, the following must be defined and estimated:

1. **Net cash flows** of the project for each year
2. **Flows connected to the investment** in each year
3. **Cost of capital (discount rate k):** depends on the cost of equity and of debt, inflation and risk
4. **Reference time horizon (T)**
5. **Terminal value (TV)**

The estimate of these elements, especially future ones, is uncertain; companies often use specialized consultants for the forecast.
