## Activity-Based Costing (ABC)

Activity-Based Costing (ABC) is a costing technique that differs from the traditional methods. It uses the concept of "activity" as the link between the resources used (and their costs) and the products that those resources help to produce. The aim is to overcome the inaccuracy of traditional systems that allocate indirect costs proportionally, offering a more precise calculation of the production cost.

**Key Points:**
- ABC is a costing system detached from the traditional structure of cost centers
- It uses "activities" to link the resources consumed to the final products
- It provides for a two-stage cost allocation process: first from resources to activities, then from activities to products
- The first stage uses "Resource Drivers" to attribute the costs of the resources to the activities that cause their consumption
- The second stage uses "Activity Drivers" to allocate the costs of the activities to the products according to their actual use
- This approach aims at a causal and more accurate attribution of costs, overcoming the proportional allocation of indirect costs typical of traditional methods

**Explanation:**
The operating mechanism of Activity-Based Costing is divided into two main phases. In the first phase, the activities that make up the production process and consume the resources (inputs such as materials, labor, overhead) are identified. The costs of these resources are then divided among the different activities through the use of "Resource Drivers", that is, indicators that explain how the activities consume the resources. In the second phase, it is analyzed how the individual goods and services use these activities. The costs accumulated in the activities are attributed to the final products through the "Activity Drivers", which are indicators that measure the actual contribution of each activity to the production of a specific product. In short, the flow is: Resource Costs → Activity Costs → Product Costs, based on a causal criterion in both steps.

**Considerations:**
- The precise identification of the activities that consume the resources, which requires a detailed description of the production process and the ability to collect accurate data on the costs of the individual activities
- The appropriate choice of the "drivers" (both Resource Drivers and Activity Drivers), that is, of the specific indicators that explain the consumption of resources by the activities (phase 1) and the use of the activities by the products (phase 2)

## Traditional Costing Techniques

The traditional costing techniques, such as Process Costing, Operation Costing and Job Order Costing, attribute production costs to goods and services with a mix of causal and proportional criteria. Their main limit is the inaccuracy in calculating the total production cost, due to the proportional allocation of indirect costs (overhead), which can lead to an incorrect determination of the margins of the products.

**Key Points:**
- **Job Order Costing:** causally attributes the costs of direct materials and direct labor, while it allocates indirect costs proportionally
- **Operation Costing:** causally attributes only the costs of direct materials, while the costs of direct labor and overhead are allocated proportionally
- **Process Costing:** used for homogeneous production, it allocates all costs (direct and indirect) proportionally
- The main problem of these techniques is the inaccuracy deriving from the proportional allocation of indirect costs, which does not reflect the actual consumption of resources by the different products
