# Lesson 3.4: Traditional Costing Techniques

## Topic Overview

The lesson of 2025-10-23 introduces and explores the main traditional product costing techniques used by management to allocate direct materials, direct labor and overhead among cost centers and products. The proportional and causal criteria are explained and three methods are analyzed in detail: process costing, job order costing and operation costing. The contexts of use, the allocation logics, operating tools such as the job cost sheet, the calculation of equivalent units and the allocation coefficients are illustrated, including actual and standard approaches. The lesson concludes with a comparative summary of the cost items that can be attributed causally and those that are allocated proportionally in each method.

---

## 1. General Concepts of Product Costing and Objectives

### Definition and purpose of product costing

- Product costing covers the practical ways in which management establishes the allocation of overhead and, more generally, of production costs among the company's goods and services
- The main purpose is to determine the unit production cost by attributing direct materials, direct labor and overhead to the different product codes, cost centers and products

### Allocation criteria: proportional vs. causal

- **Proportional methods** distribute costs with respect to quantities of the production process (e.g. machine time, number of units, labor cost) without a direct causal link between the consumption of the resource and the specific good; they are typical for indirect costs
- **Causal methods** attribute costs with a direct link between the consumption of the resource and the participation in the production process of the specific good/service; there is a type of method that allows causal attribution of indirect costs as well, covered in a later lesson

## 2. Process Costing

### Context of application and characteristics

- Used in homogeneous production processes with continuous production flows and shared successive phases; typically single-product companies in which the final output is unique
- The cost items are summed and distributed at regular time intervals on the basis of the volume of output

### Calculation of unit cost and basic assumption

- In the absence of opening and closing inventories, the unit cost is the ratio between the total costs of the process (direct materials, direct labor, overhead) and the number of units produced Q
- This assumption can be relaxed, but it serves to represent the mathematical calculation concisely

### Equivalent units: definition and calculation

- Needed when, in a cycle, not all the units started are completed; the denominator is represented as equivalent units to reflect the degree of completion
- Formally: equivalent units = number of completed units + sum of the units at the i-th stage multiplied by the degree of completion at the i-th stage

**Example:**
In the month of September, 100 units are completed and 60 units are 50% complete.

**Calculation Steps:**
- Equivalent units = 100 + (60 × 50%) = 130

### Consideration of opening and closing inventories

- Taking into account the degrees of completion of the opening and closing inventories, it is possible to calculate consistent equivalent units for each sub-interval of time and to reflect the share of accumulated costs

## 3. Job Order Costing

### Context of application and structure of jobs

- Used in organizations whose output can be represented in internally homogeneous units or lots (jobs), referring to different product codes that pass through several departments (e.g. three departments A, B, C)
- Direct materials and direct labor can be attributed causally to the individual jobs; overhead cannot be attributed directly

### Operating document: Job Order Record / Job Cost Sheet

- A sheet that records, for each job/product code, all the cost items incurred during the processing phases
- It keeps track of the number of units produced in each phase, directly attributes direct materials and direct labor; the indirect costs are referred to the departments and then allocated to the jobs through a coefficient

### Procedure for allocating indirect costs

- For each department a specific allocation base is defined (machine time, number of units, direct labor cost, number of workers, etc.)
- **Allocation coefficient for department i:** total overhead of the department / value of the allocation base of the department
- **Overhead allocated to job j:** department coefficient × use of the allocation base by job j
- With the allocated values the total cost of the job is completed and, dividing by the units of the lot, the unit cost is obtained

### Actual vs. standard (normalized) approaches

- **Actual allocation:** uses the actual indirect costs of the period to calculate precisely the share of overhead allocated to each job; its main disadvantage is the information delay due to the lag between incurring the costs and the valuation
- **Standard allocation:** uses a standard allocation coefficient based on budgeted overhead and budgeted allocation base; its advantage is timeliness, its disadvantage is potential divergence from real values, especially if process efficiency departs from the standard

---

## 4. Operation Costing

### Hybrid nature and key differences from Job Order Costing

- Intermediate system between job order and process costing; it applies when only the cost of the direct materials per lot can be identified causally, while labor and indirect costs contribute jointly in the departments
- **Difference:** in job order costing, direct materials and direct labor are identified causally; in operation costing only direct materials are causal

### Conversion cost and allocation

- **Conversion cost per department:** sum of labor cost and indirect production costs
- This conversion cost is allocated proportionally among the lots by choosing an adequate allocation base for each department, determining the coefficient and applying it as in the proportional criteria

### Comparative summary of the methods

- **Job Order Costing:** direct materials and direct labor attributed causally; overhead allocated proportionally
- **Operation Costing:** direct materials attributed causally; direct labor and overhead (as conversion cost) allocated proportionally
- **Process Costing:** all cost items allocated proportionally; in single-product contexts with continuous flows, proportional allocation makes it possible to determine precisely total cost and unit cost (total costs / equivalent units)
