# Lesson 1.2: Corporate Governance

## Topic Overview

Corporate governance, the General Meeting and the Board of Directors, variable remuneration. The lesson illustrates the separation between ownership and control in large companies and the resulting problems of alignment between shareholders and management. It presents alignment tools such as variable remuneration and corporate governance systems. It describes the roles and responsibilities of the General Meeting, the Board of Directors, the Board of Auditors and the audit firm, as well as the traditional, dualistic and one-tier models in joint-stock companies (S.p.A.), focusing on functions, appointments, controls and decision-making processes.

---

## Key Points Summary

1. Distinction between ownership and control in large companies: owners contribute risk capital but do not manage operations; managers run the daily and strategic activity
2. Goal alignment problem between owners and managers in companies where ownership and control are separate, in order to reduce the risk of diverging objectives
3. Two alignment tools (often complementary): 1) variable remuneration of managers linked to the company's economic results, encouraging the maximization of economic results; 2) corporate governance system (a set of rules and governing mechanisms)
4. Corporate governance: the set of mechanisms that regulates how a company is governed, defining roles and relationships among the different stakeholders (shareholders, creditors, employees, suppliers, buyers, etc.) and ensuring that managers follow certain rules and procedures when making decisions. It arises to solve the misalignment of objectives between ownership and control
5. Four aspects regulated by corporate governance: 1) definition of the long-term strategy and future development of the company (owners/General Meeting); 2) administration/executive management, which implements the strategy through short-, medium- and long-term decisions to carry out the corporate purpose (Board of Directors); 3) control over management, supervising laws, bylaws, principles of sound administration and the adequacy of the organization, administration, accounting and internal controls (Board of Auditors); 4) accounting control/audit, verifying the regular keeping of the accounts and the correct representation of management events in official documents such as the financial statements (audit firm)
6. General Meeting of shareholders: the body that represents the owners with a deliberative function, defining the company's long-term strategic direction
7. Board of Directors: executive function, responsible for translating the company's strategies into concrete actions through short-, medium- and long-term operating decisions; it prepares the financial statements and submits them to the General Meeting for approval, reporting annually on the performance of management
8. Board of Auditors: body that controls management, appointed by the General Meeting, made up of three or five standing members plus any alternates; it supervises compliance with the law, the bylaws and the principles of sound administration, and the adequacy of the organizational, administrative and accounting structure
9. Audit firm: body in charge of accounting control, verifying the regular keeping of the accounts and the correct representation of management events in official documents (financial statements)
10. Ordinary General Meeting: convened at least once a year, within four months of the end of the financial year (e.g. year-end on 31 December → convened by 30 April); it approves the annual financial statements, decides on the distribution of dividends, appoints/removes the Board of Directors and the Board of Auditors, and resolves on the liability of directors and auditors in case of disputes

**Key Quote:**
> "If managers have part of their salary depending on the company's results, their objective will be to maximize the company's economic result."

## Separation Between Ownership and Control in Companies

In large companies the owners (shareholders) contribute risk capital but do not manage operations; managers run the company day to day and make strategic and executive decisions. This creates a potential misalignment of objectives between ownership and management.

**Key Points:**
- **Owners:** contribute equity/risk capital and hold ownership
- **Managers:** workers responsible for daily guidance and strategic decisions
- **Small companies:** ownership and control often coincide
- **Large companies:** distinction between ownership and control, with the risk of diverging objectives

**Explanation:**
The lesson introduces the structure of the company by distinguishing roles. In small businesses, the owner decides and operates. As complexity grows, functions are separated: managers administer with decision-making autonomy. This separation can lead managers to prefer different objectives (e.g. growth in size, prestige, minimizing personal risk) over the maximization of the economic result expected by the owners. The alignment problem arises from information asymmetries and incentives that are not perfectly aligned.

**Considerations:**
- Clarity of roles between owners and management
- Recognize the risks of diverging objectives in large companies

## Alignment Mechanisms: Incentives and Corporate Governance

Two complementary tools to align the objectives of managers and owners: (1) variable remuneration linked to company results; (2) corporate governance as a set of rules, roles and controls.

**Key Points:**
- **Variable remuneration:** part of managers' salary depends on the economic result
- **Rationale:** encourage the maximization of the economic result, which is the owners' objective
- **Corporate governance:** defines relationships among stakeholders and decision-making procedures
- **Not mutually exclusive:** often used together

**Explanation:**
Linking pay to results creates direct incentives. Corporate governance structures checks and balances through bodies with defined responsibilities (General Meeting, Board of Directors, Board of Auditors, audit), reducing opportunistic behavior and ensuring compliance with laws and bylaws.

**Considerations:**
- Define clear performance metrics for the variable component
- Establish formalized rules and responsibilities in corporate bodies

## Corporate Governance: Definition, Scope and Stakeholders

Corporate governance regulates how the company is governed: roles, responsibilities, decision-making rules and relationships among stakeholders. It arises from the problem of the separation between ownership and control.

**Key Points:**
- Defines relationships among shareholders, creditors, employees, suppliers, buyers, etc.
- Assigns specific responsibilities to each subject through rules
- Objective: reduce misalignments and ensure proper management

**Explanation:**
The governance framework is an institutional response to the agency risk. It outlines who decides what, how the work is monitored, how the financial statements are prepared and approved, and who supervises compliance with laws and bylaws.

**Considerations:**
- Clarity of the bylaws and internal procedures
- Separation of functions between execution and control

## The Four Areas Regulated by Corporate Governance

Governance establishes: (1) definition of the long-term strategy; (2) administration/management; (3) control over management; (4) accounting control.

**Key Points:**
- **Long-term strategy:** assigned to the owners through the General Meeting
- **Administration:** responsibility of the Board of Directors
- **Control over management:** Board of Auditors
- **Accounting control:** audit firm/statutory auditor

**Explanation:**
Each area has a dedicated body with its own powers and responsibilities. The separation balances decision-making and the effectiveness of controls, mitigating the risk of abuse or management errors.

**Considerations:**
- Avoid overlapping competences
- Establish timely information flows between bodies

## General Meeting of Shareholders: Ordinary and Extraordinary

The General Meeting represents the owners and has strategic deliberative functions; it can be ordinary (annual) or extraordinary (no fixed schedule, for specific matters).

**Key Points:**
- **Ordinary General Meeting:** at least once a year, within four months of the end of the financial year (e.g. year-end on 31 December → by 30 April)
- **Ordinary functions:** approves the annual financial statements; determines dividends; appoints/removes the Board of Directors and the Board of Auditors; resolves on the liability of directors and auditors in case of disputes
- **Extraordinary General Meeting:** amendments to the bylaws; capital increase through new shares; issue of bonds/debt securities
- **Bank financing:** can be approved by the Board of Directors without an extraordinary General Meeting

**Explanation:**
The financial statements are prepared by the Board of Directors and approved by the General Meeting. Dividend distribution concerns the share of profits allocated to the shareholders. Extraordinary matters affect the legal and financial structure and require specific quorums and procedures.

**Example:**
If the financial year ends on 31 December, the ordinary General Meeting must be convened by 30 April at the latest to approve the financial statements, resolve on dividends, appoint any bodies and deal with any liability issues.

**Calculation Steps:**
- Identify the year-end date: 31 December
- Calculate the deadline: within four months → 30 April
- The Board of Directors prepares the financial statements and submits them to the General Meeting
- Resolutions on dividends, appointments, liability

**Considerations:**
- Compliance with formal deadlines (four months)
- Clarity on matters of ordinary vs. extraordinary competence

**Special Circumstances:**
- If the bylaws need to be amended, how should the company proceed? Convene an extraordinary General Meeting with the appropriate agenda
- If the company intends to issue bonds, how should it proceed? Convene an extraordinary General Meeting to resolve on the issue

## Board of Directors (BoD): Powers and Delegations

Executive body that implements the strategy through operating decisions; it approves significant transactions, prepares the financial statements, reports to the General Meeting, and can delegate powers.

**Key Points:**
- Examines and approves the most significant transactions from an economic, equity and financial point of view
- Prepares the financial statements and submits them to the General Meeting for approval
- Reports annually to the General Meeting on the performance of management (at the ordinary General Meeting)
- Appoints the chairperson from among its members, unless the General Meeting designates one at the time of appointment
- May delegate powers to one or more managing directors or to internal committees

**Explanation:**
The distinction between main decisions (BoD) and ordinary ones (operating management) serves efficiency. Delegations to a managing director allow speed and management continuity, while ultimate responsibility remains with the BoD.

**Considerations:**
- Clear definition of what constitutes a "significant transaction"
- Formalization of delegations and of the controls on delegations

---

## Managing Director (CEO)

Member of the BoD with delegated powers, even on matters typically reserved to the BoD, for the daily management of economic, financial and equity aspects.

**Key Points:**
- Delegations can be extensive (even "partly or entirely" covering the powers of the BoD)
- Frequent role in large companies, with operational responsibilities
- Reports to the BoD within the internal control system

**Explanation:**
The CEO concentrates operational execution, while the BoD retains direction and oversight. The system of delegations must ensure decision traceability and compliance with limits and policies.

**Considerations:**
- Set spending limits and subject-matter limits in the delegations
- Provide for periodic reporting from the CEO to the BoD

---

## Board of Auditors: Composition and Functions

Body that controls management, appointed by the General Meeting; made up of three or five members plus alternates; it supervises the law, the bylaws, sound administration and the adequacy of the organizational, administrative and accounting structure.

**Key Points:**
- **Composition:** three or five standing members plus any alternates
- **Appointment:** by the General Meeting of shareholders
- **Typical competences:** legal, economic, financial, commercial
- **Supervision of:** compliance with laws and bylaws; principles of sound administration; organizational, administrative and accounting adequacy
- **Meeting frequency:** at least once every three months; often monthly

**Explanation:**
The board can flag organizational issues (functions, divisions, accounting procedures) that hinder the company's functioning. The meeting frequency supports continuous and timely control.

**Considerations:**
- Document the checks on the organizational structure and internal controls
- Coordinate with the audit to avoid unnecessary duplication

**Special Circumstances:**
- If organizational issues emerge that hinder proper functioning, how should it intervene? The board formally reports to the BoD, proposing corrective measures and monitoring their implementation

---

## Audit Firm and Accounting Control

Body in charge (appointed by the General Meeting) of verifying the regular keeping of the accounts and the correct representation in official documents, in particular the financial statements.

**Key Points:**
- Verifies the accounting recording of management events
- Checks the correct representation of the economic, financial and equity situation in the financial statements
- Common element to the traditional, dualistic and one-tier models

**Explanation:**
The audit provides external assurance on the reliability of the information, complementing but not replacing the Board of Auditors' control over management.

**Considerations:**
- Independence and rotation of the engagement according to the applicable regulations

## Governance Models in S.p.A.: Traditional, Dualistic, One-Tier

For joint-stock companies, the legal system provides three models: traditional, dualistic and one-tier. The lesson illustrates the main characteristics of each.

**Key Points:**
- **Traditional:** the General Meeting appoints the BoD and the Board of Auditors; the General Meeting approves the financial statements, appoints/removes directors and auditors, brings liability actions against directors and auditors, and approves amendments to the bylaws; the audit firm is appointed by the General Meeting (a common element to all governance systems for accounting control)
- **Dualistic:** the General Meeting appoints the Supervisory Board; the Supervisory Board appoints the Management Board; the Supervisory Board is responsible for approving the financial statements, appointing the administrative body and bringing liability actions against directors; the General Meeting neither approves the financial statements nor brings actions against directors
- **One-tier:** described, although less widespread in Italy

**Explanation:**
The traditional model creates a dualism between the BoD (executive) and the Board of Auditors (control over management), with a separate external audit. The dualistic model concentrates the approval of the financial statements and the appointment of the management body in the Supervisory Board, changing the prerogatives of the General Meeting. The one-tier model, although described, is less adopted by Italian companies.

**Considerations:**
- Clarity of the General Meeting's competences in each model
- Adapt the bylaws and practices to the chosen model

## Corporate Governance Systems: Traditional, Dualistic, One-Tier

Overview of the three corporate governance models provided for, distinguishing the bodies involved (legislative, executive, control over management, accounting control), appointment methods, distribution of powers and levels of organizational simplification.

**Key Points:**
- **Traditional:** General Meeting of shareholders (legislative), Board of Directors or sole director (executive), Board of Auditors (control over management), audit firm or Board of Auditors (accounting control)
- **Dualistic:** General Meeting of shareholders (legislative, with fewer powers than in the traditional model), Management Board (executive) appointed by the Supervisory Board, Supervisory Board (control over management, takes on some of the General Meeting's powers), accounting control as required by law (often an audit firm)
- **One-tier:** General Meeting of shareholders (legislative, with powers as in the traditional model), Board of Directors (executive and seat of control over management through an internal committee), internal control committee within the BoD
- In all three models, accounting control can be carried out by the audit firm or by the Board of Auditors; in joint-stock companies whose shares are partly distributed on the market, accounting control is always entrusted to an audit firm
- **Increasing simplification:** traditional (baseline), dualistic (General Meeting focused on appointing the Supervisory Board), one-tier (control inside the BoD, easier relations between executive and control)
- The one-tier system is little adopted by Italian companies
- In the traditional model it is possible to replace the BoD with a sole director

**Explanation:**
The instructor illustrates the three systems placing the General Meeting of shareholders as the legislative pivot. In the traditional model, the General Meeting retains broad powers (bylaw amendments, profit distribution, appointment/removal of control bodies) and appoints the BoD or a sole director; the Board of Auditors supervises management, while accounting control is preferably carried out by an audit firm, which is mandatory in S.p.A. whose shares are widely distributed on the market. In the dualistic model, the General Meeting, with more limited powers than in the traditional model, appoints the Supervisory Board; this appoints the Management Board and controls management, absorbing some of the General Meeting's powers and interfacing with the executive body it appointed itself. In the one-tier model, the General Meeting keeps the typical powers of the traditional model, but the BoD includes an internal committee for control over management, simplifying and facilitating the relationship between the executive function and the control function; although leaner, this model is little adopted by Italian companies.

**Example:**
An S.p.A. whose shares are partly distributed on the market keeps the General Meeting of shareholders with full powers, and appoints a Board of Directors and a Board of Auditors for control over management. Accounting control is mandatorily entrusted to an audit firm. The General Meeting approves bylaw amendments, resolves on profit distribution and can remove the auditors.

**Calculation Steps:**
- Definition of the bodies: General Meeting, BoD, Board of Auditors, audit firm
- Verification of the external audit requirement given the distribution of the shares
- Decision-making process: approval of the financial statements and profit distribution at the General Meeting
- Appointment and removal mechanisms for the control bodies

**Dualistic model example:**
The General Meeting of shareholders, with reduced duties compared to the traditional model, appoints the Supervisory Board. The Supervisory Board appoints the Management Board and controls management, taking on some of the General Meeting's powers. The relations between the supervisory body and the executive body are central to the effectiveness of the model.

**Calculation Steps:**
- Configuration of the bodies: General Meeting, Supervisory Board, Management Board
- Transfer of some powers from the General Meeting to the Supervisory Board
- Appointment of the Management Board by the Supervisory Board
- Information and control flows between supervision and executive

**One-tier model example:**
The General Meeting of shareholders keeps the typical powers of the traditional model and appoints a Board of Directors. Within the BoD an internal committee for control over management is set up. This solution simplifies the relations between those who execute (BoD) and those who control (internal committee), but is little adopted by Italian companies.

**Calculation Steps:**
- Definition of the bodies: General Meeting, BoD, internal control committee within the BoD
- Advantages: simplification and smoother relations between executive and control
- Weaknesses: low adoption and a possible risk of lower perceived independence of the control

**Considerations:**
- Verify the obligation to entrust accounting control to an audit firm in S.p.A. whose shares are widely distributed on the market
- Assess the degree of independence of the control when choosing between Board of Auditors, Supervisory Board and internal committee
- Consider the desired organizational simplification when comparing the models (traditional vs. dualistic vs. one-tier)
- Clearly define the residual powers of the General Meeting in the dualistic and one-tier models
- Choose between BoD and sole director in the traditional model based on the size and complexity of the company
- Ensure effective information flows between the executive body and the control body in all systems
- Provide for the possibility of appointment and removal of the members of the bodies by the General Meeting where envisaged

**Special Circumstances:**
- If the shares are partly distributed on the market, how should accounting control be handled? Always entrust it to an audit firm
- If the company wants to reduce the complexity of its bodies, which model should it choose? Consider the one-tier model for simplification and the dualistic model for a clear separation between management and supervision
- If the company does not want a large BoD, how should it proceed in the traditional model? Appoint a sole director
